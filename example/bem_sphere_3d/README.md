# Three-dimensional sphere BEM

This example solves the unit-sphere Laplace Dirichlet problem with the P0
Galerkin boundary-element method and compares its capacitance with the exact
value \(4\pi\). It also applies the finest-mesh single-layer operator through
both the dense matrix and the pointer-free hierarchical tree. The same
dense-versus-hierarchical comparison is performed for the complex outgoing
Helmholtz kernel. A retained-tree FortNum GMRES solve is compared with the
dense Galerkin solution and the analytical outgoing radial sphere field.
At the interior Dirichlet resonance \(k=\pi\), dense and retained-tree
combined-field solves are compared with the exact radial field
\(\exp(i\pi(r-1))/r\).

The gallery leads with `sphere_exterior_solution_3d.png`, a physical field
sampled on a smooth observation shell outside the sphere.  The shell is
slightly radially modulated so the decaying monopole (u=1/r) is visible in
one 3-D view; colored points are direct evaluations of the BEM representation,
not source data or an abstract convergence quantity.

Generated outputs:

- `sphere_exterior_solution_3d.png`: computed exterior (1/r) field on the
  connected observation shell, with a filled tensor-product surface and
  ordered samples (the first gallery stage);
- `sphere_exterior_solution_3d.csv`: ordered `(theta,phi)` surface samples
  containing coordinates, computed values, exact `1/r`, and absolute error;
- `gallery_sequence.txt`: machine-readable stage order proving that the
  physical solution precedes density, operator, and convergence diagnostics;
- `sphere_capacitance.png`: Galerkin capacitance convergence;
- `sphere_hierarchical_action.png`: dense and hierarchical operator actions;
- `sphere_helmholtz_hierarchical_action.png`: real and imaginary outgoing
  Helmholtz operator actions;
- `sphere_helmholtz_solve.png`: analytical, dense, and hierarchical outgoing
  fields along a radial line;
- `sphere_helmholtz_cfie_resonance.png`: analytical, dense, and hierarchical
  combined-field solutions at the first interior sphere resonance;
- `sphere_helmholtz_cfie_scaling.png`: dense quadratic interaction counts
  versus retained-tree counts over 128, 512, and 2048 panels;
- `benchmark.txt`: panel counts, dense and hierarchical solve times, GMRES
  iterations and residual, field and density errors, and interaction counts.

The matrix-free tree follows the geometric acceptance principle of
Barnes and Hut, *Nature* 324 (1986), doi:10.1038/324446a0. Exact Galerkin
self-panel entries are retained; only far clusters satisfying both geometric
separation and a low-frequency \(k a\) criterion use monopoles.
The restarted complex GMRES implementation is owned by FortNum; the cluster
tree and exact self terms are built once and retained across all matvecs.
The resonance-safe representation is the three-dimensional combined-field
construction of Brakhage and Werner, *Archiv der Mathematik* 16 (1965),
doi:[10.1007/BF01220037](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01220037). Its
second-kind numerical formulation follows Lin, *SIAM Journal on Numerical
Analysis* 22 (1985), doi:[10.1137/0722041](https://doi.org/10.1137/0722041).
Topological near-panel single- and double-layer blocks are integrated once
with tensor-Duffy quadrature and retained across GMRES matvecs. On the plotted
512-to-2048-panel interval, the measured interaction-count exponent is about
0.83, while a dense operator grows with exponent 2.

The plots are generated by FortPlot in CI and are intentionally not committed.
